What do the A1, A2, B1, B2, C1 and C2 levels mean?

Language levels are defined by the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR). It's used worldwide, in courses, exams and hiring.

Three groups, six levels

The CEFR splits levels into three groups: A (basic user), B (independent user) and C (proficient user). Each group has two levels.

LevelWhat you can do
A1 BeginnerIntroduce yourself, ask and answer simple questions, understand familiar words and very simple sentences.
A2 ElementaryTalk about yourself, your family and work, go shopping, describe the past in simple sentences.
B1 IntermediateHandle most situations when travelling, describe experiences and plans, explain your opinion.
B2 Upper intermediateTalk easily with native speakers, understand the main ideas of complex texts, debate.
C1 AdvancedUnderstand long, demanding texts and speak fluently and flexibly at work and in study.
C2 MasteryUnderstand almost everything and express yourself precisely, with nuance, on any topic.

Which level do I need?

  • For travel, A2–B1 is enough.
  • For work, B2 is usually expected.
  • For study abroad, often B2 or C1.
