French pronunciation for beginners: 7 rules

In French many letters are written but not pronounced. These seven rules will help you read correctly from your first week.

1. The final consonant is usually silent

Paris sounds like "pa-ree", petit like "p'tee", beaucoup like "bo-koo". Common exceptions are c, r, f and l: avec, bonjour, neuf, sel. A handy way to remember them is the English word CaReFuL.

2. A final e is usually silent

Madame, France. But é (with an accent) is always pronounced: café.

3. Nasal vowels

When a vowel is followed by n or m (and no vowel after that), the vowel goes through the nose and the n isn't heard separately: an/en (enfant), on (bonjour), in (vin). Armenian, Russian and English don't have these sounds, so they're best learned by listening and repeating.

4. The French r

The French r is made at the back of the throat, close to the Armenian «ղ», not with the tip of the tongue. Merci, Paris, rouge.

5. ou and u are two different sounds

ou is like "oo" in "food": vous. u doesn't exist in English: say "ee" and round your lips as if for "oo": tu, rue. Vous and vu are different words.

6. Liaison: linking words

A silent final consonant sometimes wakes up when the next word starts with a vowel: les amis sounds like "lay-za-mee", vous avez like "voo-za-vay". Here the s sounds like z.

7. Letter combinations

  • oi → "wa": moi
  • au, eau → "o": beau
  • ch → "sh": chat
  • gn → "ny": champagne
  • ai → "e" as in "bed": lait

Stress

In French the stress always falls on the last syllable of a word or phrase, just like in Armenian. That's a nice head start for Armenian speakers.
