How to become a polyglot: 8 practical tips

You don't need a special talent to become a polyglot. You need the right order, a daily habit and a bit of patience.

What is a polyglot?

"Polyglot" comes from Greek: poly ("many") and glōtta ("tongue, language"). It means someone who speaks several languages well. There's no official threshold, but people usually mean four languages or more.

Many people in Armenia are already halfway there: they grow up with Armenian and Russian, and often English too. Every new language gets easier, because you already know how learning a language works.

1. Start with one language

Starting two new languages at once often leads to mix-ups, especially similar ones like Spanish and Italian. A good rule is to bring your first new language to about B1 before starting the next.

2. Use language families

After Spanish, Portuguese and Italian feel much easier because so many words are similar. French belongs to the same Romance family. The right order saves months.

3. Every day, even 20 minutes

Short daily sessions beat three hours once a week. Your brain remembers words and structures best through repetition.

4. Listen a lot

Listening is the foundation. Start with recordings at your level: first without the text, then with it. Our listening platform is built exactly this way.

5. Review at intervals

Review a new word after a day, three days, then a week. This method (spaced repetition) helps you keep words for years.

6. Speak from day one

Mistakes are part of learning. The sooner you start speaking, the sooner the "language barrier" disappears.

7. Set a clear goal

"Learn Spanish" is too vague. "Reach B1 in six months" or "pass the A2 test" is a goal. Use the CEFR levels to measure your progress.

8. Test yourself

Check your level regularly. To begin, take one of our free tests: Spanish, English or French.

In short

Becoming a polyglot is a marathon, not a sprint: one language at a time, a daily habit, lots of listening and speaking. At Verba School we teach Spanish, English and French with explanations in Armenian.
